# Veylattice World Bible (Concept Draft)

**Status:** Proposed canon direction. Nothing in this document is final until reviewed and accepted. Build systems so names and lore can change without code rewrites.

## Core premise
The known world was fractured by a cataclysm called the Sundering, leaving five elemental currents woven through geography, living beings, and ancient machines. Vessels are warriors, beasts, mystics, constructs, spirits, and stranger beings able to manifest these currents in arena combat. Arenas are historical locations where one or more currents have become unusually concentrated.

## The five elements
- **Wood:** renewal, memory, living architecture, old guardians, patient growth. Visual language: carved timber, moss, roots, jade-like light, organic silhouettes.
- **Fire:** ambition, destruction, transformation, volcanic kingdoms, dangerous invention. Visual language: obsidian, ember seams, brass, angular flame shapes.
- **Earth:** endurance, duty, shelter, buried cities, oathbound defenders. Visual language: layered stone, ochre minerals, broad silhouettes, monumental geometry.
- **Metal:** precision, order, engineered life, disciplined legions. Visual language: silver alloy, dark steel, rivets, crisp edges, restrained luminous accents.
- **Water:** adaptation, secrets, memory carried by currents, maritime and submerged cultures. Visual language: deep blue glass, flowing forms, pearl-like highlights, weathered silver.

These are cultural themes, not personality restrictions. Any element can produce compassionate, cruel, comic, scholarly, or conflicted characters.

## Historical locations and arenas
Every arena entry should include: name, region, short history, elemental affinity, passive rule, terrain layout, hazard timing, counterplay, and a visual palette. Environmental effects must have clear visual telegraphs and affect both players symmetrically unless an explicitly asymmetric scenario is introduced later.

## Character and relationship lore
Use relationships that have narrative reasons: comrades, siblings, rivals, former teacher/student, commander/soldier, creator/creation, sworn guardians, or beings connected by a shared historical event. Mechanical synergy is a consequence of the relationship, not the sole reason it exists. Avoid treating every character as connected to every other character.

## Current named locations (concepts)
- **Emberfall Caldera:** A failed attempt to forge a weapon capable of controlling all five currents left a volcanic wound in the land. Fire-affinity units are empowered; lava channels create telegraphed hazards.
- **Drowned Reliquary:** A submerged archive where memories are stored in mineralized water. Water-affinity units are favored; selected flooded tiles affect grounded movement.
- **Verdant Crucible:** An overgrown sanctuary that reclaims ruins and remembers every life that passed through it. Wood-affinity units are favored; living terrain can provide limited defensive benefits.
- **Ironwake Foundry:** An abandoned war-engine complex that still responds to old commands. Metal-affinity units are favored; marked machinery can obstruct movement or briefly alter lanes.
- **Shattered Crownlands:** A fractured highland of collapsed fortresses. Earth-affinity units are favored; stone formations create cover and choke points.
- **Astral Breach:** A region where reality periodically destabilizes. No permanent elemental affinity; marked cells change on a visible, predictable schedule.
- **Confluence Garden:** A neutral arena with several small elemental zones. It rewards varied decks without giving one element total control.

## Lore consistency rules
1. Do not present proposed concepts as finalized canon without approval.
2. Card flavor text, bestiary entries, arenas, and bonds should not contradict one another.
3. Original names, lore, dialogue, visuals, and mechanics only. Do not copy protected characters, lore, card text, assets, or distinctive presentation from existing games.
4. Keep lore concise enough to support gameplay; the player should not need to read a novel to understand a card.
5. Do not use appearance as a substitute for cultural depth. Each faction and region needs internal variation.
